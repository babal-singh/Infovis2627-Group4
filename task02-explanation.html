Our dashboard is designed for Marta Puig, a 52-year-old Senior Traffic Safety and Urban Mobility Coordinator at the Ajuntament de Barcelona. She holds a degree in Civil Engineering and Urban Planning and has more than 15 years of experience in public service.

Marta is an expert in her domain: she knows Barcelona's street network, local traffic regulations and the city's usual high-risk spots. However, her statistical and visualization knowledge is basic. She understands trends, rates and categories, but she has neither the time nor the training to work with statistical models, code or raw spreadsheets. For this reason, the dashboard must show what is happening, where and when at a glance, using familiar chart types and simple measures such as counts and percentages.

She will use the dashboard on a desktop computer, since she works from her office and needs enough screen space to compare several charts at once. She will consult it weekly, on Monday or Tuesday mornings, to plan the week's interventions and prepare her report to the council.

Her goal is to understand where, when and why accidents happen, how severe they are, and whether the situation is improving.

A typical scenario would be the next: every Monday morning, Marta has about an hour before the weekly mobility committee. Instead of going through raw files, she opens the dashboard and, in a few minutes, identifies the most dangerous areas, days and hours, and the behaviours behind the most serious accidents.With this information she decides how to manage the available resources, such as patrols, inspections or infrastructure reviews, and she brings the charts to the meeting as evidence for her proposals.
